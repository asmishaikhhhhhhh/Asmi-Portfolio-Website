## 2. Structured extraction

- **One-liner:** The cover gives only "CLAP IT" plus tags. The portfolio line (from the brief) is "Ergonomic board pins that secure papers without damage, combining comfort, grip, and organization." The images contain no other tagline.
- **Problem:** "Minimize the damage caused by the board pins on paper and hold the paper rigid in place" (02). Problems mapped in 02: holes on paper, tears (paper thickness, wind), creases (pin pressure), angle/force, rusting, unorganizable packaging, easily lost (small size, roll away), exposed tip pokes, wrong angle makes paper fall, multiple papers, and safety (falls on floor and pokes shoe soles/foot; pokes hands while picking up).
- **Users:** Students (projects, exhibitions); Children (crafts); Teachers, Project Heads, Organizations, Securities (updates, notices, statistics reports, monthly targets) (02).
- **Research/observations:** The mind map (02) covers push pin types, parts (head/body), materials (plastic, wood, steel, brass), paper thickness (GSM) and sizes (A4, A3, A5, Newsprint), bulletin board materials (cork with hexagonal cell structure, sponge), holding position photos, conventional and unconventional uses, and ways to pin (hole through paper = holds strong/rigid; side of paper = to reduce damage). It includes photos of real paper damage. Research method, sample size and interviews: NOT STATED.
- **Ergonomic considerations:** Design concerns are Easy Push Pull, Strong Grip, Definite, Better Accuracy and Safety (03). The sketches note grooves for better grip, easy pinch grip, soft edges to minimise crease, and an extended needle for visible accuracy (03). The final design has a "Cap for better grip while opening" (07). It is shown held in a pinch grip and is 2.7cm tall (10). The images make no stated anthropometric data and give no ergonomic rationale beyond these notes.
- **Ideation/sketches:** The low-fidelity sketch sheet (03) shows about 9 concepts: a grooved pinch pin, a wedge head with an extended needle, a thumb disc, a diamond-head pin, a magnet N/S clip, adhesive discs ("safe to use, sticks everywhere, doesnot damage the paper"), a bent-wire clip, a pin dispenser, and a pen-style pusher.
- **Iterations:** Low-fi sketches (03), then mid-fi physical mock-ups (04: a paperclip on a block, and a foam pinch wedge with a needle), then an early CAD concept (05: a white pill top view and a wood-textured side with a slot and pin), then the final CAD (06: a black D-shaped hinged clamshell). Reasons for moving between iterations: NOT STATED.
- **Final form:** A black D-shaped (rounded-bottom, flat-top) two-part hinged clamshell. Magnets on the inner faces clamp the paper, and a small cap/tab helps with opening (01, 06, 07). It is 2.7cm tall (10). Base and lid thickness are 5.00 mm, and the face length is about 20.066 mm with a 16.00 mm magnet span (09).
- **Materials (CMF, 08):** Rubber, Stainless Steel, PLA. Colour is HEX 242422 / rgb(36, 36, 34) / hsl(60, 3%, 14%). Finish is semi-gloss. The image does not explicitly say which part uses which material (see the note under 08).
- **Mechanism:** The paper sits between two magnets in the hinged halves, which hold "paper of any GSM without damaging the paper" (07). There are three interchangeable or variant back mounts: a magnet for magnetic boards, a suction cup for smooth glossy surfaces, and a pin for soft or cork boards (07). Whether the backs are interchangeable or separate SKUs: NOT STATED.
- **Renders/prototype:** Renders are in 01, 05, 06, 07 and 08. The tool is listed as Blender 3D in the metadata (the slides do not name a render tool). Mid-fi physical prototypes are in 04. A physical prototype of the final form is tested in 11 (its fabrication method is NOT STATED; it looks textured, possibly 3D-printed, but this is unconfirmed). Image 10 hand shots appear composited (unconfirmed).
- **Testing:** "MID-FIDELITY PROTOTYPES & TESTING" (04) and "USER TESTING" (11) show photos only. Image 11 shows the prototype holding a sheet on a pinboard without a hole through the paper (a small mark is visible in the middle photo, but its meaning is NOT STATED). An unlabelled 4.5-of-5 squares graphic appears in 11 and its meaning is NOT STATED. No participants, method, metrics or feedback are stated.
- **Learnings:** NOT STATED.
- **Duration:** 75 hours (01).
- **Year:** Published Aug 2025 (Behance metadata). Project year: NOT STATED in the slides. A damage photo in 02 shows a paper reading "Year III Sem VI", which suggests an academic context, but this is unconfirmed.
- **Tools:** Photoshop, Blender 3D (Behance metadata). The slides do not name the CAD software used for the dimensions drawing in 09.
