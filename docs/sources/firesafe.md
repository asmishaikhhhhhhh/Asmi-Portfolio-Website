## 2. Structured extraction (only what is visible)

- **One-liner (from deck, img 25):** "FIRE SAFE enhances traditional field training methods by embedding them with cutting-edge immersive technologies." Also (img 26): immersive simulation lets trainees experience complex emergency scenarios, tactical procedures and high-risk environments "without the real-world danger."
- **Context / domain (img 01, 02, 03, 05, 11):** Firefighter training, chosen for "Critical Safety needs", "Technological Advancements", "Impact Potential". Focus user is the TRAINEE. Setting: Mumbai / BMC fire brigade (35 fire stations; govt allots 200-300 crores/year to BMC fire brigade dept; recruitment = aptitude test, physical test, 6 months training with 3k/month stipend till positioned). Research objective targets "experienced Indian firefighters".
- **Current training flow (img 05 to 07):** 600 hours total = 420 practical + 120 theory + 60 employability skills. 24-week curriculum in 6 blocks of 4 weeks (full list in images 06 and 07).
- **Problem statement (img 20):** "How might we create a supplement for firefighter trainees that enhances their situational awareness and decision making skills in controlled environments before they enter real-world scenarios?"
- **Problems faced by trainees (img 13):** 6-month to 1-year wait for situational training (happens in big urban stations); officers rarely use equipment and rely on theory; trainees struggle to track equipment and functions daily; "Watch and learn" policy (trainees don't use equipment); selection on fitness followed by IQ.
- **Target users:** Firefighter trainees (primary); station officers also interviewed. Personas (img 18, 19): "Arjun Kumar", 22, trainee from Mumbai, 4th month of training; and (also named "Arjun Kumar") 35, Station Officer, 8 years in Mumbai fire service, leads team of 6-8. Business customer segments (img 45): trainees and academies, government/municipal fire departments, private fire safety organisations, oil and gas industries.
- **Research methods and numbers:**
  - Literature review: 39 research papers + 21 articles (img 04); themes emergency response simulation, cognitive load, decision-making, firefighter training.
  - Primary interviews: 35 total = 25 trainees + 10 station officers (img 10).
  - Ecosystem map (img 11), personas (img 18, 19).
  - Usability testing: "UT RESULTS" with "8+" (img 44); what "8+" counts is NOT STATED.
- **Key stats (exact, with source image):** 39 research papers, 21 articles (04); 600 h / 420 h / 120 h / 60 h (05); 24 weeks (06, 07); 35 interviews: 25 trainees, 10 station officers (10); 35 fire stations, 200-300 crores/year, 3k/month stipend, 6 months training, 4 levels (11); 6 months to 1 year wait (13); "8+" (44). App mock values (28) and dashboard values (43) are UI placeholder data, not research findings.
- **Secondary findings (img 08):** communication realism gap in immersive training; live-fire training infrequent due to cost and safety; immersive systems fail to simulate physical demands and psychological pressure; limited budgets mean unequal training quality for smaller departments.
- **Primary findings (img 12):** 5 findings (lack of visual representation in drills; individual skills lag and commanders struggle to visualise/coordinate; training-to-deployment gap with loss of theory knowledge; seniors refresh knowledge via equipment/procedure reviews; trainees assist rather than participate until probation ends).
- **Insights (img 14 to 17):** live fire training (rare, costly, risky); knowledge gaps (senior officers hesitant about immersive tech); psychological development (decision-making under stress, cognitive load, emotional regulation); controlled environments (need development pathway from training to field).
