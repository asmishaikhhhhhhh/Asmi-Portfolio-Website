## Research question
- Starting question: why do news channels and newspapers run daily zodiac predictions, often in the early morning? The article also asks how these predictions, whether believed or just entertained, shape routines, decision priorities and emotional states.
- Three aims:
  1. Examine how astrology and celestial motifs are represented across modern media.
  2. Understand how different demographic groups perceive and respond to these portrayals.
  3. Analyse whether media representations affect personal beliefs or daily practices.
- Stated challenges: interpretations vary widely; media influence is hard to isolate from other factors; cultural variation; participant bias; ethics around privacy and emotional impact.

## Methods
1. Topic selection: several sub-topics were shortlisted and briefly assessed for importance, knowledge and interest.
2. Observation: she watched morning news programs on several channels, noted the tone, format and timing of zodiac segments, and observed viewers' verbal and body-language reactions.
3. Interviews: one-on-one interviews with people who regularly engage with predictions, covering reasons for consuming them, beliefs about accuracy, sign perceptions and effects on decisions. Group discussions explored differences by gender.
4. Content analysis: adjectives used for each sign across news broadcasts, newspapers, websites, magazines and books.
5. Comparative analysis: media descriptions compared with public perceptions.
- Structure: Set A = content analysis (Ch. 1–2); Set B = interviews and discussions (Ch. 3); Set C = comparative analysis (Ch. 4).
- A literature review cites Bibik 2009, Marsden 2017, Wu 2013, Anderson 2015, Kellner & Share 2007, O'Neill 2015, and Ryan & Wynveen 2017.

## Key findings
- Media descriptions share common adjectives (e.g. creative, ambitious, adventurous, practical, emotional) but differ by source. Tone also varies by sign: Libra is portrayed positively, while Scorpio sometimes gets negative descriptors.
- Public perceptions largely matched media adjectives. Personal experiences with people of a sign added nuance and could flip the descriptors (e.g. the Capricorn example).
- Age: younger people were more open and used signs as conversation starters and in dating choices. Older people were more sceptical and treated signs as entertainment.
- Gender: some participants applied gender stereotypes to signs. Women showed more interest in romantic compatibility. Men in the group discussions were more sceptical.
- Intersection: younger women were most likely to use zodiac signs in daily life and self-reflection. Older men were most likely to dismiss them as superstition.
- Three belief clusters: Believers (signs as guidance), Enthusiasts (a substantial portion; entertainment), Sceptics (a smaller, vocal group; pseudoscience).
- Comparative analysis: media and public adjectives overlap substantially, but public perceptions are richer and shaped by experience. Five suggested reasons explain why people of the same sign are perceived differently by gender: socialisation and gender norms, individual variation, expression vs perception bias, self-fulfilling prophecy, and interpersonal dynamics.

## Key takeaways / conclusion
- Astrology in media works more as entertainment and cultural identity than as rigid belief.
- Engagement spans Believers, Enthusiasts and Sceptics.
- Age, gender and culture shape how astrology is received.
- Media visibility widens astrology's reach, but the audience's own interpretation gives it meaning.
- Conclusion: astrology in media creates shared cultural symbols and a playful language for identity, relationships and destiny, even among sceptics. The article ends with a reflective question to readers about whether they are engaging with belief, entertainment or culture.
