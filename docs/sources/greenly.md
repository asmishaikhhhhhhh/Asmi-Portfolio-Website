- **09** — Gap Analysis (6 cards): 1. Limited local manufacturing capabilities: There is a lack of affordable, India-specific equipment and solutions that suits small and midsized farms, as most products are designed for global markets. 2. Awareness and education gap: A significant portion of the population is unaware of the benefits of hydroponic produce, limiting mass adoption and market positioning. 3. Lack of cost-effective solution development in vertical farming: Current vertical farming solutions are expensive and provide inconsistent yield quality, making scaling production difficult in limited land area, affecting small-scale hydroponic farmers. 4. No standardized interface for all sensors: Even though the sensors can be remotely accessed they can only be individually accessed through separate applications which increases production cost due to higher manual labour. 5. Lack of network infrastructure in hydroponic farming ecosystem: Farmers lack a network infrastructure to find reliable equipment and raw material suppliers, as well as for finding interested customers (B2B and D2C) which reduces productivity and profitability. 6. Workers hired are not knowledgeable in hydroponics: Limited knowledge and training on hydroponic techniques are significant barriers in meeting productivity targets. Workers who are knowledgeable are very expensive to hire.
